# 1. CALIFORNIA — petition "expungement" + the biggest automatic system in the country

**Terminology:** California has no true expungement. The petition remedy is **dismissal/set-aside** under PC § 1203.4 (probation cases), § 1203.4a (no-probation misdemeanors/infractions), § 1203.41/.42 (felony/realignment cases) — colloquially "expungement." The automatic layer is **PC § 851.93 (arrests) + § 1203.425 (convictions)** — DOJ reviews statewide databases monthly and grants relief without any petition.

**Automatic relief (the headline):** AB 1076 (2019) → SB 731 expansion, operative **October 1, 2024** after two delays (AB 134 pushed to Jul 2024; AB 168 to Oct 2024 — encode the final date). Criteria: not a § 290 registrant, no active supervision, nothing pending, conviction on/after Jan 1, 1973. Misdemeanors: 1 yr after judgment ⚠️ verify sub-criteria; probation cases upon completion; SB 731 extended relief to additional felonies (generally 4 yrs post-sentence for non-serious/non-violent ⚠️ verify the exact felony tiers in current § 1203.425(b)). Effect: DOJ notation + courts barred from disclosing (functional sealing since Aug 2022 for ALL set-asides, past and future). **The DOJ's own overview page (oag.ca.gov) is the primary source.**
UI posture: "your record may already be clear — here's how to check" (DOJ fingerprint record review, $25) → petition only if the automatic system missed you or you're outside its criteria.

**Petition dismissal (PC 1203.4):** probation completed + no new offense + nothing pending → relief **as of right**; discretionary in-the-interests-of-justice otherwise. 1203.4a: 1 yr post-conviction for no-probation misdemeanors. 1203.41/.42: felony jail/prison realignment cases (SB 731 expanded 1203.41 to most felonies incl. prison sentences, 2 yrs post-completion ⚠️ verify). Exclusions: certain sex offenses, some vehicle code. **No filing fee ⚠️** — recent sources say none statewide (AB 1076-era fee elimination); older county schedules charged ~$120–150 — a perfect confirm-kill call. Prosecutor 30-day response; most decided without hearing.

**Arrest sealing:** § 851.91/.87 petition ("sealing as a matter of right" for non-convictions) where 851.93 automation missed.

**Personas:** (1) misdemeanor 2019, no probation issues → likely ALREADY auto-relieved — check-record path. (2) felony w/ probation completed 2021 → 1203.4 as of right (or auto). (3) prison-term felony (non-violent) done 2020 → SB 731 path. (4) § 290 registrant → excluded; honest-no. (5) arrest, never charged, 2018 → 851.93 auto or 851.91 petition.

**Verify:** oag.ca.gov automatic-record-relief page · PC 1203.4/.41/.425 + 851.91/.93 current text · courts.ca.gov cleaning-record self-help · one superior court clerk (LA) on the no-fee question.

# 2. ARIZONA — your home state; three separate remedies, no automation

*(Drafted from the 7/14 corrected rules — verify against the statutes below with the same rigor as the other 49.)*

**Three distinct tracks — the tree must not blur them:**
1. **Set aside (§ 13-905)** — conviction vacated, released from penalties, but **stays on the public record** with a "set aside" notation. Apply to the sentencing court after sentence completion (fines/restitution paid); no statutory waiting period. Exclusions: dangerous offenses, § 13-3821 registrable offenses, victim under 15, certain driving-on-suspended. Includes **Certificate of Second Chance** (added 2021) for qualifying set-asides — restores licensing opportunities. ⚠️ Filing fee: Maricopa packet shows none — confirm on the Session 1 call.
2. **Sealing (§ 13-911, eff. Jan 1, 2023)** — the newer, stronger remedy: case records hidden from public view; petitioner may deny the record in most contexts. Waits from sentence completion ⚠️ encode from statute text: class 2–3 felonies 10 yrs / class 4–6 felonies 5 yrs / class 1 misdemeanors 3 yrs / lower misdemeanors 2 yrs (+ prior-felony bump); excludes dangerous offenses, sex/registrable offenses, victim-under-15 offenses, serious offenses per § 13-706. Petition in the court of the case; prosecutor/victim response window ⚠️ verify; separate petition per case.
3. **Marijuana expungement (§ 36-2862, Prop 207)** — true expungement for possession/consumption/transport ≤2.5 oz, paraphernalia, ≤6 plants; petition anytime, free, mandatory grant if within scope.

**No automatic relief of any kind in AZ** — every track is petition-based. UI copy should say so plainly (users hear "clean slate" news from other states and assume).

**Personas:** (1) class 6 felony possession, done 2018 → set aside now + sealing eligible 2023+ → both. (2) marijuana possession 2015 → § 36-2862 free expungement. (3) DUI misdemeanor → set aside OK ⚠️ verify; sealing excluded? — resolve from § 13-911 text. (4) dangerous offense → neither; honest-no. (5) dismissed charge 2020 → § 13-911 sealing of non-conviction ⚠️ verify immediate availability.

**Verify:** §§ 13-905, 13-911, 36-2862 current text · Maricopa CRSA + sealing packets (superiorcourt.maricopa.gov) · Session 1 call answers (fees, processing) · azcourts.gov sealing pages.

# 3. NEW YORK — Clean Slate is LAW but the backlog is still sealing

**Terminology:** sealing (NY has no general adult expungement; only MRTA cannabis records were expunged). Two pathways:

1. **Clean Slate Act (CPL § 160.57, eff. Nov 16, 2024):** automatic sealing — misdemeanors **3 yrs**, felonies **8 yrs**, from sentencing or release from incarceration (whichever later); clock resets on new conviction; must be off probation/parole ⚠️ verify supervision condition; excludes sex offenses (Arts. 130/263) and **Class A felonies EXCEPT drug offenses** (Art. 220 A-felonies ARE sealable — a surprising, sourced detail). **THE implementation fact: OCA has until November 16, 2027 to seal the pre-existing backlog.** As of July 2026 the system is mid-rollout — many eligible old records are NOT yet sealed. UI copy: "eligible ≠ sealed yet; here's how to check, and the petition path below may be faster." Post-2027 remedy: petition for review if your record was missed.
2. **Petition sealing (CPL § 160.59, 2017):** 10 yrs from sentence/release; max **2 convictions, at most 1 felony** (same-transaction crimes may count as one); discretionary (interests of justice), DA served, possible hearing; excludes sex offenses, violent felonies (§ 70.02), Class A felonies, homicide. **No filing fee ⚠️ verify.** For people outside Clean Slate's classes or tired of waiting for the backlog, this is live now.

**Non-convictions:** CPL 160.50/.55 — dismissals/acquittals seal automatically at disposition (long-standing). **Cannabis:** MRTA expunged qualifying marijuana convictions automatically (2021) — check-record.

**Personas:** (1) misdemeanor 2019, sentence done → Clean-Slate-eligible NOW but possibly not yet processed → check via DCJS record review, petition 160.59 as backup? (10-yr wait not met → wait for auto) — the nuanced flagship persona. (2) drug Class A felony, released 2015 → Clean-Slate-eligible (the surprise). (3) violent felony → excluded both paths; honest-no. (4) two misdemeanors 2010 → 160.59 petition now OR wait for auto — cost/speed tradeoff copy. (5) dismissal → already sealed at disposition.

**Verify:** CPL 160.57/160.59 text · nycourts.gov Clean Slate implementation page (rollout status — the call question) · DCJS record-review process · Legal Aid Society / LawNY Clean Slate guides.

# 4. TEXAS — expunction vs nondisclosure, freshly renumbered

**Terminology:** **expunction** (destruction — Ch. 55A, Code of Criminal Procedure, **recodified from Ch. 55 effective Jan 1, 2025** — the 7/14 catch, now confirmed; older forms/guides citing Ch. 55 are stale) vs **orders of nondisclosure** (sealing — Gov't Code Ch. 411, Subch. E-1). The bright line: **convictions are essentially never expungable in Texas** (except pardon); the conviction/deferred remedy is nondisclosure.

**Expunction (55A):** acquittal (⚠️ NEW: 55A creates automatic expunction at acquittal — trial court orders it then and there; verify scope); pardon; dismissal without community supervision (incl. certain automatic-dismissal pathways ⚠️ verify); no-charge arrests after waits (Class C 180 days / A-B 1 yr / felony 3 yrs, or SOL expiry); grand-jury no-bill; identity theft; completed Class C deferred; completed pretrial diversion. Result: records destroyed; lawful denial.

**Nondisclosure (Gov't Code 411.071 et seq.) — a lattice of specific sections; encode per-section:**
- § 411.0725: deferred adjudication (non-conviction) — most misdemeanors immediate on discharge; misdemeanors under Penal Code chs. 20–22, 25, 42, 43, 46, 71 → 5 yrs; felonies → 5 yrs
- § 411.0735: certain misdemeanor convictions → wait after completion ⚠️ verify current period (sources split 2 vs 5 yrs — encode from statute)
- DWI paths (§§ 411.0726/.0731/.0736): first-offense DWI, BAC <0.15, no accident w/ another person, no CDL → **2 yrs with full-term ignition interlock, 5 yrs without** ⚠️ map the right section numbers
- Disqualifiers per section: violent/sex/DV/trafficking offense history kills most paths
**Fees ⚠️:** expunction = civil filing fee, county-set (~$280–$400 range commonly cited) + per-agency service costs; nondisclosure = civil filing fee + $28 statutory fee — phone targets both.

**Personas:** (1) arrest, no charges, felony-level, 2022 → 3-yr wait → eligible 2025 (or SOL). (2) deferred adjudication misdemeanor theft, discharged 2024 → nondisclosure now-ish. (3) misdemeanor CONVICTION → 411.0735 path only, never expunction — the expectation-setter. (4) first DWI, interlock full term, done 2023 → nondisclosure 2025. (5) acquitted last month → automatic expunction at acquittal ⚠️ — confirm it actually happened; if not, petition.

**Verify:** Ch. 55A + Gov't Code 411 Subch. E-1 current text · Texas State Law Library expunction guide (the authority — and they answer reference questions) · TexasLawHelp forms · one district clerk (Harris) on both fee stacks.
