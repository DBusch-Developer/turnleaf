# 1. FLORIDA — the restrictive giant

**Terminology:** court-ordered **sealing** (§ 943.059) vs **expunction** (§ 943.0585). Sealing = withheld-adjudication and some non-convictions; expunction = dismissed/never-charged (or records sealed 10+ yrs). FDLE **Certificate of Eligibility required BEFORE any petition** — like Utah's BCI gate, but stricter.

**The two rules that decide almost everything:**
1. **One court-ordered seal-or-expunge per LIFETIME** (§§ 943.0585/943.059). Prior FL relief = permanently ineligible. (Out-of-state prior relief no longer disqualifies, since 2013.)
2. **Any adjudication of guilt on the FL record — for ANY criminal offense, ever — bars the certificate.** Florida does not seal or expunge convictions, period. Withheld adjudication is the only conviction-adjacent outcome that can be sealed, and a long list of offenses (§ 943.0584: DV battery, sex offenses, lewd offenses, trafficking, etc.) can't be sealed even with adjudication withheld.

**Tracks:** (a) Expunction — charges dismissed/nolle prossed/no-filed → certificate → petition; (b) Sealing — withheld adjudication on a non-disqualified offense, sentence complete → certificate → petition; (c) 10-year rule — sealed 10 yrs → may petition to expunge; (d) **Administrative/automatic sealing (§ 943.0595, 2019)** — FDLE auto-seals qualifying non-judicial arrest records that ended in non-conviction ⚠️ verify current scope and status on FDLE's page; (e) niche tracks: lawful self-defense expunction (§ 943.0578), human trafficking, juvenile diversion.

**Process/fees:** FDLE application ($75 money order, non-refundable) + notarized form + fingerprints + certified disposition; expunction applications also need Section B completed by the State Attorney. Processing ~12 weeks (FDLE's own figure). Then petition in the county of arrest; **clerk filing fee varies by county ⚠️** (~$42–$60 range commonly cited — phone target). Certificate valid 12 months ⚠️ verify.

**Draft tree:** prior FL seal/expunge ever? → ineligible (lifetime rule) → any adjudicated-guilty conviction on FL record? → ineligible for certificate → route to "check the niche tracks / legal aid" → disposition fork (dismissed → expunction path; withheld adjudication → disqualified-offense check → sealing path; adjudicated → ineligible) → sentence complete → result: eligible-to-apply-to-FDLE / ineligible / complex.

**Legal aid:** county legal aid orgs; many State Attorney offices publish walkthroughs; Florida Justice Center.

**Personas:** (1) charge dropped 2020, no other record → expunction path. (2) withheld adjudication, grand theft, sentence done, clean → sealing path. (3) adjudicated misdemeanor 2010 → ineligible (conviction bar) — the honest-no persona. (4) sealed a case in 2015 → ineligible (lifetime rule). (5) withheld adjudication, DV battery → ineligible (disqualified offense despite withhold).

**Verify list:** fdle.state.fl.us Seal & Expunge section (COE instructions, FAQ, denial reasons, § 943.0595 auto-sealing page) · Fla. Stat. §§ 943.0585, 943.059, 943.0584 (Online Sunshine) · one county clerk's seal/expunge page for the petition fee.

# 2. ILLINOIS — the generous giant, with a 2-week-old change

**Terminology:** **expungement** (destroy — non-convictions, supervision, qualified probations) vs **sealing** (hide — most convictions). 20 ILCS 2630/5.2.

**⚠️ FRESH LAW:** the **Illinois Clean Slate Act** (signed Jan 16, 2026) began phasing in **June 30, 2026** — two weeks ago: misdemeanor/ordinance sealing wait dropped **3 yrs → 2 yrs**, and a prior felony conviction no longer automatically bars a later felony sealing petition. The Act's AUTOMATIC sealing system comes later ⚠️ — verify the automatic-start date on ILAO's Clean Slate FAQ before writing any "automatic" copy for IL. Until then: petition-only state.

**Expungement (non-convictions):** arrests w/o conviction, acquittals, dismissals — generally immediate; completed court supervision — 2 yrs (5 for some offenses ⚠️ verify list); qualified probation (410/TASC etc.) — 5 yrs.

**Sealing (convictions):** most misdemeanors and Class 1–4 felonies — **3 yrs from last sentence completion (2 yrs for misdemeanors as of June 30, 2026)**. Immediate for non-convictions and after certain education milestones (diploma/degree during the wait ⚠️ verify — the education-waiver provision is real and great UX). Exclusions: DUI (absolute), reckless driving (unless under 25), domestic battery, violations of protection orders, sex offenses/registry, animal-cruelty offenses. Subsequent-felony unsealing risk (changing under Clean Slate ⚠️ verify current text).

**Cannabis:** its own universe — minor cannabis arrests auto-expunged by ISP (780k+ done), convictions pardoned via PRB/Governor process or vacated on State's Attorney motion; New Leaf Illinois provides free representation.

**Process/fees:** file in the circuit court of the county of the case (Cook: any district or the Expungement Department at Leighton); statewide Easy Forms via Illinois Legal Aid Online; e-filing statewide; **filing fee varies by county ⚠️ (Cook charges one fee for all petitions filed the same day — get the number)**; fee waiver available; objection window for ISP/State's Attorney ~60 days.

**Legal aid:** Illinois Legal Aid Online (Easy Form is genuinely excellent — link it), Cabrini Green Legal Aid, New Leaf hotline, Cook County Adult Expungement Advice Desk (Daley Center, M/W 9–12).

**Personas:** (1) misdemeanor theft, sentence done 2023 → eligible NOW under the 2-yr change (was 3!) — the fresh-law persona. (2) Class 4 felony possession, done 2021 → eligible-sealing. (3) DUI → never sealable. (4) supervision completed 2023 → expungement 2025+. (5) old felony + new felony → post-June-30 rules ⚠️ resolve during verification.

**Verify list:** 20 ILCS 2630/5.2 current text (ilga.gov — note the July 1, 2025 version split) · illinoislegalaid.org Clean Slate Act FAQ + sealing/expungement guides · cookcountyclerkofcourt.org expungement pages (fees) · ISP expungement pages.

# 3. OHIO — two remedies since SB 288

**Terminology:** since Apr 4, 2023 (SB 288), **sealing** (hide) and **expungement** (destroy) are DISTINCT remedies with different waits. Most users want sealing; expungement is the longer-wait upgrade. R.C. 2953.31–.34.

**Sealing waits (from FINAL DISCHARGE = sentence + probation/parole + fines + restitution paid; court costs don't count):**
- Minor misdemeanor: 6 months · Misdemeanors: 1 yr · F4/F5: 1 yr · F3 (where eligible): 3 yrs
- Dismissals/acquittals/no-bills: immediate, no limits (2953.33)

**Expungement waits:** misdemeanors 1 yr (minor 6 mo); felonies **10 yrs after the date they became sealing-eligible** (so F4/F5 ≈ 11 yrs total).

**Eligibility structure (SB 288 killed the "eligible offender" cap — per-conviction analysis now):** F1/F2 never; F3 blocked if the person has more than one other felony (or the 2-F3+2-misd+more pattern); OVI and all traffic never; offenses of violence; sex offenses w/ registry; victim under 13; DV convictions (M4 DV sealing narrowly allowed); protection-order violations. ⚠️ One secondary source claims an HB 1 (2023) cap of "up to 5 felonies / 3 F4+" — the Ohio Supreme Court's own June 2026 bench card and CCRC describe offense-specific rules with the F3 count limits instead. **Encode from the bench card + statute, not the secondary.** Court discretion remains: judge weighs applicant's interest vs government need; prosecutor gets 60 days to object; hearing 45–90 days after filing.

**Extras worth a tree node:** prosecutor-initiated sealing for low-level drug offenses (2953.39); Certificates of Qualification for Employment (CQE) as the fallback for the ineligible; human-trafficking expungement anytime.

**Process/fees:** apply to the sentencing court (common pleas for felonies, municipal for misdemeanors); **fee commonly $50 ⚠️ but set by court schedules — phone target**; one application can cover multiple cases in that court.

**Legal aid:** Ohio Justice & Policy Center (their March 2025 handout is the best plain-language guide), Ohio Legal Help, county law library guides (Franklin County's is strong).

**Personas:** (1) M1 theft, final discharge 2024 → eligible-sealing 2025 → likely eligible now. (2) F5 drug possession, discharged 2023 → sealing-eligible 2024; expungement ~2034. (3) F2 → never; CQE path. (4) OVI → never; honest-no. (5) F3 + one other felony → blocked (count rule) — verify against bench card.

**Verify list:** codes.ohio.gov R.C. 2953.32 current text · supremecourt.ohio.gov Adult Rights Restoration bench card (June 2026 rev — your primary) · ohiojpc.org 2025 handout · one clerk of courts fee page (Hamilton/Franklin).

# 4. GEORGIA — "record restriction," and the vocabulary IS the product

**Terminology:** Georgia does not "expunge." The remedy is **record restriction** (GCIC criminal history hidden from non-criminal-justice access) plus **court-record sealing**. O.C.G.A. § 35-3-37. Using Georgia's words in the UI is credibility.

**Tracks:**
1. **Non-convictions:** arrests on/after **July 1, 2013** that end without conviction → restricted **automatically** by GCIC ⚠️ (with documented reporting gaps — UI should say "should be automatic; verify your GCIC report"). Pre-2013 arrests → apply to the arresting agency (fee varies by agency ⚠️).
2. **Misdemeanor convictions (SB 288 "Second Chance," eff. Jan 1, 2021):** petition the court of conviction to restrict AND seal **up to 2 misdemeanor convictions lifetime**, **4 yrs** after sentence completion, no new convictions in the 4 yrs, no pending charges. Excluded: DUI, family-violence battery (unless under 21 at arrest), sex offenses, crimes against minors, serious traffic offenses (§ 35-3-37(j)(4)(A) list).
3. **Felonies:** pardon from the State Board of Pardons and Paroles first (no serious violent/sex felonies), then petition to restrict+seal. Plus First Offender Act (deferred adjudication, once, judge-approved) and retroactive First Offender for old cases.
4. Survivors First Act track (trafficking survivors): vacate or restrict+seal.

**Process/fees:** petition in the court of conviction, serve the prosecutor, possible hearing; **no statewide fee — county court costs vary ⚠️ phone target**. GCIC applies restrictions within weeks of the order.

**Legal aid:** **Georgia Justice Project** (gjp.org — THE resource; they run statewide clinics and wrote the law), Atlanta Legal Aid, GA Legal Services Program.

**Personas:** (1) arrest 2019, charges dismissed → should be auto-restricted → check-GCIC path. (2) two misdemeanor shoplifting convictions, done 2020, clean → eligible to petition for both (lifetime max reached after). (3) DUI misdemeanor → excluded. (4) nonviolent felony 2012, clean since → pardon path (Board of Pardons), then petition. (5) arrest 2010, dismissed → pre-2013 → apply to arresting agency.

**Verify list:** O.C.G.A. § 35-3-37 current text · gjp.org record-restriction FAQ (the practitioner-grade summary) · GBI/GCIC record restriction pages (process + pre-2013 application) · one county clerk for petition costs.

# 5. NORTH CAROLINA — the most category-heavy statute in the country, with a fresh 2025 cut

**Terminology:** "expunction" (NC's statutory word; also says expungement). A dozen-plus separate statutes; the two your tree needs most: **§ 15A-146** (dismissals/not guilty) and **§ 15A-145.5** (nonviolent convictions). UNC School of Government's Relief guide is the authoritative reference.

**⚠️ FRESH LAW:** S.L. 2025-71 (petitions on/after **July 9, 2025**) — the current statute text now reads **3 years for one nonviolent misdemeanor** (was 5). Most guides online still say 5. Encode 3, cite the current § 15A-145.5(c)(1)(a) text, and note the effective date — another states-where-Turnleaf-is-more-current-than-Google entry.

**Convictions (§ 15A-145.5), waits from conviction or sentence completion (whichever later), conviction-free during the wait:**
- 1 nonviolent misdemeanor: **3 yrs** (2025 change)
- Multiple nonviolent misdemeanors: **7 yrs** from last conviction/sentence end
- 1 nonviolent felony: **10 yrs** (**15 yrs** for felony breaking & entering under 14-54(a))
- 2–3 nonviolent felonies: **20 yrs**, and the felonies must have been committed within a **24-month window**
- Prior 145.5 expunction limits apply (misd-then-more-misd generally barred; check (c4)/(c5) legacy clauses)

**"Nonviolent" excludes:** Class A–G felonies, Class A1 misdemeanors, any assault-element offense, registry offenses, listed sex/stalking offenses, meth/heroin/PWISD-cocaine felonies, CMV felonies, DWI (absolute), attempts at any of the above. Outstanding restitution blocks.

**Non-convictions (§ 15A-146):** dismissals/not-guilty on/after Dec 1, 2021 → **automatic expunction by operation of law, 180–210 days after disposition** — paused Aug 2022, **resumed July 8, 2024** under SB 565 ⚠️ verify it's still running; plea-agreement dismissals are NOT automatic (petition instead); older non-convictions → petition, no waiting period, no prior-conviction bar, free (except deferred-prosecution dismissals).

**Process/fees:** petition (AOC-CR-281/287/298 series) in the county of conviction; DA notice + victim notice for 145.5; judge may require SBI/FBI record check; **$175 filing fee for conviction expunctions ⚠️ verify current — waived for indigent petitioners**; non-conviction petitions generally free.

**Legal aid:** NC Second Chance Alliance (ncsecondchance.org — statewide clinics), NC Justice Center (their Summary of NC Expunctions is the practitioner bible), Legal Aid of NC.

**Personas:** (1) one nonviolent misdemeanor, done 2022 → eligible NOW under the 3-yr change (was ineligible until 2027 under old guides!) — the fresh-law persona. (2) dismissal in 2023 → should be auto-expunged → check-record. (3) DWI → never. (4) one Class H felony, done 2014 → eligible (10 yr). (5) two nonviolent felonies from 2004 and 2009 → committed >24 months apart → NOT eligible for multi-felony expunction — the trap persona.

**Verify list:** ncleg.gov § 15A-145.5 + § 15A-146 current text · UNC SOG Relief from a Criminal Conviction microsite (updated for 2025 changes — your primary) · ncsecondchance.org · one clerk of superior court for the $175 fee.
