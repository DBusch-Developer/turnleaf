# 1. WASHINGTON — "vacate," and the court file stays public

**Terminology:** Washington does not expunge or (generally) seal. Convictions are **vacated** — plea withdrawn/verdict set aside, charges dismissed. RCW 9.96.060 (misdemeanors) / RCW 9.94A.640 (felonies). After vacation you may lawfully say you were never convicted, and WSP stops reporting it — **but the court file remains public** (sealing under GR 15 is separate and rare). That honesty note belongs in the WA result copy; competitors blur it.

**New Hope Act (2019, amended 2021/2024):** broadened eligible offenses; **2024: the waiting clock no longer waits for LFO payoff** — it runs from release/sentencing, and courts can waive or reduce outstanding LFOs on motion. Older guides still say "pay all fines first" — encode the 2024 rule ⚠️ verify the session-law cite.

**Misdemeanors (9.96.060):** 3 yrs since the later of release from supervision/confinement or sentencing; no new convictions in the prior 3 yrs; no pending charges; sentence terms complete (LFO nuance above). DV-related offenses: 5-yr track with extra conditions (no restraining-order violations in prior 5 yrs; fewer than two separate-incident DV convictions ⚠️ verify the exact DV subsection). Never vacatable: DUI/physical control (46.61.502/.504), obscenity/child-exploitation offenses, most sex offenses, violent offenses. Prior-3-yr protection-order violations disqualify. Special tracks: trafficking/DV-victim convictions (9.96.080 / 9.94A.648, expedited); marijuana misdemeanors (21+ at offense — mandatory vacate); pre-1975 treaty-fishing convictions.

**Felonies (9.94A.640):** must first have a Certificate of Discharge (9.94A.637). Class C: 5 yrs since discharge + no new crime in prior 5 yrs. Class B: 10 yrs + no new crime in prior 10 yrs. Class A: never. Violent offenses/crimes against persons: never — EXCEPT (2019) Assault 2, Assault 3 (non-officer), Robbery 2 vacatable if no firearm/deadly-weapon/sexual-motivation enhancement. Felony DUI: never.

**Process/fees:** motion in the sentencing court (county forms; Pierce and King publish complete packets; statewide forms on courts.wa.gov); ⚠️ filing fee — one guide says generally none; counties may differ — phone target. WSP WATCH self-check $11 online (free in person). No general automatic vacation; WSP updates + FBI notification flow from the clerk's transmittal.

**Personas:** (1) misdemeanor theft, sentenced 2020, LFOs still owed → eligible NOW under 2024 rule (old guides say no) — fresh-rule persona. (2) Class C felony possession, discharged 2019, clean → eligible. (3) Class B felony 2018 → 2028. (4) DUI → never; honest-no. (5) Assault 2, no enhancement, discharged 2015 → eligible under the 2019 carve-out — the surprise-yes persona.

**Verify:** app.leg.wa.gov RCW 9.96.060 + 9.94A.640 current text · courts.wa.gov vacating/sealing forms page · washingtonlawhelp.org vacate guides (2024-updated) · one county clerk packet (Pierce's is thorough).

# 2. TENNESSEE — expunction with a brand-new TBI gate

**Terminology:** "expunction" — actual destruction of public records. T.C.A. § 40-32-101. ⚠️ **2025 statutory reorganization**: content is being renumbered into §§ 40-32-106/107 — cite both old and new until settled; the AOC site itself says "updated information coming soon."

**Non-convictions:** dismissals, nolle, no-bill, not guilty, arrest-no-charge → expunction **free** (the statute declares legislative intent that no fee EVER be charged) and available anytime. Trap: charged with multiple counts and convicted of ANY count from the same episode → the rest generally can't be expunged (§ (a)(1)(E)). Diversion completions (pretrial/judicial) expungable — fee applies.

**Convictions (§ (g)):** single eligible conviction — most misdemeanors + listed Class E felonies (theft, forgery, credit-card fraud, some drug possession...), **5 yrs** after sentence completion, all fines/costs/restitution paid, no other convictions. **Newer tier ⚠️: certain Class C and D felonies, 10 yrs** — added recently; most older guides only mention E — verify the current (g)(1)(D)–(F) list. Excluded: A/B felonies, listed C felonies, sexual offenses, DUI (Title 55 ch.10 pt.4), vehicular assault.

**Two-conviction path (§ (k)):** up to 2 offenses — two misdemeanors, or one eligible felony + one misdemeanor — each independently (g)-eligible, both before any ineligible conviction, 5 yrs (10 if a C/D felony is involved), **once per lifetime**. Same-episode multiples count as one.

**⚠️ NEW GATE (Jan 1, 2024):** no conviction-expunction order may be entered without a **TBI certificate of eligibility** attached — TBI certifies the offense qualifies (not the person; the court still decides). Adds a step + processing time to every conviction track — verify the request process and turnaround on TBI's site.

**Process/fees:** petition in the court of conviction; DA served (certified mail on some tracks, 20-day objection on (f); general practice varies). **No state fee; clerk may charge up to $100** (§ 8-21-401(b)(1)(D)(x)) for conviction/diversion expunctions; indigency affidavit waives. Dismissals: free, period.

**Personas:** (1) dismissed charge 2021 → free expunction now. (2) misdemeanor theft, sentence done 2019, paid → (g) eligible. (3) Class E theft + misdemeanor, both done 2015 → (k) two-offense path — once ever. (4) DUI → never. (5) Class D felony, done 2013 → the new 10-yr tier ⚠️ — resolve during verification.

**Verify:** current § 40-32-101 (+106/107) text · tncourts.gov/expungements (forms + the reorganization note) · TBI certificate process page · Davidson County Criminal Court Clerk for the ≤$100 practice.

# 3. MASSACHUSETTS — the mail-in state (best UX in the country)

**Terminology:** **sealing** (CORI hidden; §§ 100A–100C) vs **expungement** (permanent destruction; §§ 100E–100U, narrow). The headline: **conviction sealing is administrative — one form, by mail, free, no court, and NON-DISCRETIONARY** once the wait is met. The Commissioner of Probation must seal; no judge, no hearing, no reasons. For most users this is the whole answer.

**Administrative sealing (§ 100A), waits from disposition or release from incarceration (whichever later), reset by intervening convictions/incarceration:**
- Misdemeanors: **3 yrs**
- Felonies: **7 yrs**
- Sex offenses: 15 yrs + no longer under registration duty (registry-required = can't seal)
Form: Petition to Seal (TC-005 ⚠️ verify current form name/number) → mail to Commissioner of Probation, One Ashburton Place, Room 405, Boston 02108. **No fee.** Some offense categories ineligible for administrative sealing ⚠️ verify list (firearms licensing statutes, some state-ethics offenses).

**Court sealing (§ 100C):** non-convictions — acquittals/no-bills/no-probable-cause seal **automatically at disposition**; dismissals/nolle prosequi seal by petition to the court, discretionary ("substantial justice"), available immediately (no wait) — good for people who can't wait out 100A.

**Expungement (§§ 100E–100U), deliberately narrow:** time-based — offense before the petitioner's **21st birthday**, waits 3 yrs misd / 7 yrs felony, max 2 expunged records lifetime, no subsequent cases, ~20 excluded categories (§ 100J: ABDW, firearms, OUI, restraining-order violations, sex offenses...); Commissioner screens eligibility, court decides "best interests of justice." Non-time-based (§ 100K): false identity, offense no longer a crime, fraud on the court, error — anytime. UI framing: expungement is the exception; sealing is the product.

**Record check:** iCORI self-request $25 (fee waiver for indigent).

**Personas:** (1) misdemeanor conviction 2020, clean → mail the form NOW — the flagship persona. (2) felony conviction 2017, clean → mail the form. (3) dismissal last month → court petition under 100C, no wait. (4) offense at 19, now 26, one record → expungement candidate. (5) registry-required sex offense → can't seal; honest-no.

**Verify:** mass.gov "Seal your criminal record" + "Expunge your record" pages (forms, current addresses) · MGL c.276 §§ 100A, 100C, 100E–100U · Greater Boston Legal Services CORI self-help materials (the practitioner standard).

# 4. INDIANA — one petition, one lifetime

**Terminology:** "expungement" (IC 35-38-9), though mechanically it's sealing for lower levels and public "expunged" marking for higher felonies. The defining rule: **ONE expungement petition per lifetime (§ 9-9)** — it must include everything at once; multi-county records = separate petitions per county, all filed within a **365-day window**, and together they count as the one shot. This changes the advice fundamentally: the tree's job in Indiana is partly to tell people when NOT to file yet.

**Tracks and waits:**
- Arrests/charges w/o conviction (§ 9-1): **1 yr**, free; post-2022, dismissed-case arrests auto-expunge in defined circumstances ⚠️ verify scope; 2022 also added infraction-adjudication arrests and diversion-participant eligibility (w/ prosecutor authorization).
- Misdemeanors incl. felonies reduced to misdemeanors (§ 9-2): **5 yrs** from conviction (earlier w/ prosecutor's written consent); fines/fees/restitution paid; no new convictions in the 5 yrs → **grant is MANDATORY** if criteria met.
- Class D / Level 6 felonies not reduced (§ 9-3): **8 yrs**; mandatory grant; § 9-3 exclusion list (bodily-injury offenses, sex/violent offenders, etc. ⚠️ verify).
- Higher felonies (§ 9-4): **8 yrs** (or 3 yrs post-sentence, whichever later); DISCRETIONARY; record marked "expunged" but stays public.
- Serious felonies (§ 9-5 — serious bodily injury, elected-official misconduct, etc.): **10 yrs** AND prosecutor's written consent; discretionary; marked-not-hidden.
- Never: murder/homicide-level offenses, sex/violent-offender registry, sex crimes, official misconduct (per § 9-5(b) ⚠️ verify list).

**Process/fees:** petition in the convicting court; prosecutor 30-day objection window; possible hearing. Fees ⚠️: § 9-1 arrest petitions are free by statute; conviction petitions pay the civil filing fee (~$100 vicinity, county-set) — phone target.

**Personas:** (1) arrest, charges dismissed 2024 → free petition now (or already auto-expunged — check). (2) misdemeanor 2018, paid, clean → MANDATORY grant — but counsel-the-timing persona if they also have a 2021 Level 6 (waiting until 2029 lets one petition catch both; filing now burns the shot). (3) Level 6 felony 2015, clean → eligible, mandatory. (4) Level 4 felony 2014, clean → discretionary, marked-public — expectation-setting copy. (5) serious-bodily-injury felony → 10 yrs + prosecutor consent; honest-maybe.

**Verify:** IC 35-38-9 current text (iga.in.gov) · indy.gov Second Chance page · Indiana Legal Services expungement materials · one county clerk for the conviction-petition fee.

# 5. MISSOURI — fresh limits, presumption in your favor

**Terminology:** "expungement" = court-ordered sealing (§ 610.140 RSMo); restored to pre-offense status, lawful denial in most contexts. Exclusion-list architecture: everything is expungable UNLESS listed — **~1,900 offenses qualify**; 11 exception categories.

**⚠️ FRESH LAW (Jan 1, 2025, SB 754):** lifetime limits raised to **2 felonies + 3 misdemeanors/ordinance violations** (was 1F+2M — many attorney sites STILL show the old numbers; instant Discrepancy material); unlimited infractions. Counting change: separate crimes in one case are **no longer automatically counted as one** toward the limits (same-course-of-conduct exception nuanced ⚠️ verify current text). Arrest expungements: petition at 18 months (was 3 yrs).

**Waits (SB 53, 2021):** felony **3 yrs**, misdemeanor/ordinance/infraction **1 yr**, from completion of disposition. Clean period runs **backward from filing** (no convictions other than most traffic during the prior 3/1 yrs — Suni's guide flags this direction nuance).

**Eligibility posture:** all obligations paid; no pending charges; habits/conduct show no threat; public-welfare consistent — and when criteria (1)–(4) are met and (5)–(6) pled, there's a **rebuttable presumption FOR expungement; the prosecutor bears the burden** to defeat it. That's unusually petitioner-friendly — worth a line of UI copy.

**Excluded (§ 610.140.3):** Class A felonies; dangerous felonies (§ 556.061); death-element felonies; felony assault; ANY domestic assault; felony kidnapping; sex-registry offenses; most weapons offenses; intoxication-related traffic/boating/flying — **except a first-time DWI can be expunged after 10 yrs with no further alcohol offenses** (its own track ⚠️ verify cite, likely § 610.130/.140 interplay); CDL-related driving offenses; the long enumerated list.

**Process/fees:** petition in the court of the case; prosecutor 30-day objection; court must rule within 6 months; self-help forms at courts.mo.gov. **Fee conflict ⚠️: $250 statutory surcharge per one source vs "standard circuit filing fee" per another** — phone target; fee-waiver by in-forma-pauperis motion. Marijuana: separate automatic expungement under 2022's Amendment XIV rollout (courts still processing ⚠️ status check). Clean Slate automation bills: pending, not law ⚠️ verify nothing passed this session.

**Personas:** (1) misdemeanor stealing 2022, paid → eligible now. (2) two felonies (2015, 2018 possession), clean → BOTH expungable under the 2025 limits (impossible under old law) — fresh-law persona. (3) domestic assault misdemeanor → excluded; honest-no. (4) first DWI 2012, nothing since → the 10-yr DWI track — surprise-yes. (5) arrest 2025, no charges → 18-month track mid-2026.

**Verify:** § 610.140 current (2025) text (revisor.mo.gov) · clearmyrecordmo.org (law-change page + practitioner slides) · courts.mo.gov expungement forms · one circuit clerk for the fee conflict.
