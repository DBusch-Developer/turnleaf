# Turnleaf — Wave 5 Draft Research Package
## MD · WI · SC · AL · LA — drafted July 15, for Diana's verification

Standing rules apply. This wave's theme: **the cost and access spectrum.** Maryland charges ~$30; Alabama charges $500; Louisiana caps at $550 but just switched on an automated free-request path; South Carolina routes everything through prosecutors' offices; Wisconsin has almost no path at all. The fee number IS the story in three of these states.

# 1. MARYLAND — cheap, broad, and freshly reformed

**Terminology:** expungement (Crim. Proc. § 10-101 et seq.); shielding (MD Second Chance Act) is a separate, narrower remedy — mention, don't build.

**Non-convictions:** acquittals and full dismissals → **automatic expungement since Oct 2021** (NOT retroactive — older cases petition ⚠️ verify mechanics); nolle prosequi → petition (3-yr wait or immediate w/ general waiver ⚠️ verify); stet → 3 yrs; PBJ (probation before judgment) → 3 yrs post-discharge (PBJ is Maryland's signature disposition — the tree needs a PBJ branch).

**Convictions (REDEEM Act, eff. Oct 1, 2023) — waits from sentence completion incl. probation/parole:**
- Eligible misdemeanors: **5 yrs** (was 10)
- Second-degree assault: **7 yrs** (was 15)
- Eligible felonies: **7 yrs** generally; burglary 1st/2nd + felony theft: **10 yrs** (was 15)
⚠️ Several sites still quote the un-passed 3/5-yr version of the bill — Discrepancy material. The eligible-offense LIST (mostly nonviolent misdemeanors + a short felony list, § 10-110) was NOT expanded by REDEEM — verify the list itself.
⚠️ Waiting-clock dispute: whether "sentence completed" includes full parole/probation expiration was contested (2024 HB 73 stalled) — verify current practice.
⚠️ A "2025 Expungement Reform Act" headline exists — verify what actually passed in the 2025 session before encoding; MVLS's 2025 presentation is the check.

**The unit rule:** all charges in one case must be expungable or none are — EXCEPT cannabis charges (2023 carve-out). This rule blocks tons of Marylanders; it needs its own tree node.

**Cannabis:** simple possession convictions → petition-eligible immediately, fee-waived; possession-with-intent → 3 yrs; auto-expungement by Jul 1 2024 applied to the **CJIS database only, not court records** (courts' own tip sheet says so) — UI copy: "your state record may be clear while the court record isn't; here's the petition to finish the job."

**Process/fees:** forms CC-DC-CR-072 (conviction) / related series; file in the court of the case; **$30 per petition for convictions ⚠️ verify, waivable; non-conviction expungements free**; prosecutor 30-day objection; ~90-day completion norm.

**Legal aid:** Maryland Court Help Centers (free brief advice — statewide line on mdcourts.gov); MVLS (mvlslaw.org — the practitioner authority).

**Personas:** (1) PBJ for theft 2020, discharged → eligible now. (2) misdemeanor CDS conviction 2017 → 5-yr REDEEM wait met → eligible. (3) 2nd-degree assault 2018 → 2025+ → eligible. (4) case with one expungable + one non-expungable charge → unit-rule block; honest-no. (5) cannabis possession conviction → immediate petition, no fee.

**Verify:** § 10-105/10-110 current text · mdcourts.gov expungement pages + cannabis tip sheet · MVLS 2025 presentation (what passed in 2025) · one clerk for the $30.

# 2. WISCONSIN — the honest-no state

**The rule, and it's nearly the whole page:** Wis. Stat. § 973.015 — expungement is available ONLY if the judge ordered it **at the time of sentencing**, for offenses committed **before age 25**, with a max penalty of **6 years or less** (all misdemeanors + Class H/I felonies), excluding listed violent H felonies and others in (1m)(a)3. If the judge didn't order it then, **no petition process exists** — State v. Matasek / State v. Arberry closed the door. ⚠️ Reform failed AGAIN in 2025: Evers put a petition process in the budget (LRB-1770); the Legislature stripped it. Assembly bills passed in 2021 and 2024; the Senate never voted. Verify nothing passed in the weeks since — but as of research date, the answer is no.

**If it WAS ordered at sentencing:** completion is self-executing (State v. Hemp) — successful completion (no new convictions, probation not revoked, conditions satisfied, fines paid) triggers the discharge certificate → record expunged, no petition needed. Some people don't know their expungement already happened — a check-CCAP branch.

**The second honesty note:** WI expungement removes **court records (CCAP) only.** The DOJ Crime Information Bureau record survives; employers running CIB checks still see it. Encode that sentence.

**Everything else routes to pardon:** Governor's pardon (Evers revived the process, 2019) — felony convictions, 5 yrs post-sentence-completion, application via the Governor's Pardon Advisory Board ⚠️ verify current criteria/wait; a pardon doesn't expunge but restores rights and, since 2021 executive changes, some expedited review exists. Juveniles: § 938.355(4m) petition at 17. Trafficking survivors: § 973.015(2m) motion anytime for prostitution convictions.

**Fees:** none in statute for the at-sentencing mechanism.

**Personas:** (1) 23-yr-old, misdemeanor, judge ordered expungement, probation done → already expunged — check CCAP. (2) 23-yr-old, same crime, judge silent at sentencing → NO path; pardon only — the defining honest-no. (3) 27-yr-old at offense → never eligible. (4) Class I felony at 22, ordered, completed → expunged, but CIB record persists — expectation-setting. (5) old felony, sentence done 2015 → pardon application.

**Verify:** § 973.015 current text (docs.legis.wisconsin.gov) · confirm 2025-26 session outcome · evers.wi.gov pardon information · Wisconsin court system expungement brochure.

# 3. SOUTH CAROLINA — the solicitor runs everything

**Terminology:** expungement (Order for Destruction of Arrest Records), Uniform Expungement of Criminal Records Act, § 17-22-910 et seq. **Structural quirk: you apply through the circuit SOLICITOR'S office** (16 judicial circuits), not the court — the solicitor determines eligibility, coordinates SLED verification, and processes the order. The tree's "where do I go" answer is a prosecutor's office; that surprises people.

**Non-convictions:** summary-court (magistrate/municipal) dismissals/not-guilty → **automatic and free since June 2009** (§ 17-22-950); General Sessions dismissals/nolle pros → through the solicitor, **free if not part of a plea deal**; plea-deal dismissals pay full fees ⚠️ verify. PTI and diversion completions → expungable.

**Convictions — a closed list of specific statutes, each first-offense-only:**
- § 22-5-910: first-offense conviction with penalty ≤30 days/$1,000 → **3 yrs** clean from conviction (DV 3rd degree: 5 yrs)
- § 22-5-920: Youthful Offender Act convictions (17–24) → **5 yrs** post-sentence, non-violent, no registry, **once per lifetime**; retroactive YOA path for pre-2010 convictions
- § 22-5-930: first-offense simple possession / PWID drugs → **3 yrs** (conditional-discharge 10-yr lookback quirk)
- § 34-11-90(e): first-offense fraudulent check → 1 yr
- § 56-5-750(F): first-offense failure to stop for blue lights → 3 yrs
⚠️ The general first-offense-nonviolent bill (§ 17-22-915 — would add a broad 3-yr path) has been filed repeatedly (H.4602, H.3730) and **was still NOT law as of research date** — verify session status; if it passed, SC's page changes fundamentally.
Everything else — most felonies, DUI, DV 1st/2nd, violent, sex — → **pardon only** (Probation, Parole & Pardon Services board; pardons in SC don't expunge but remove many barriers).

**Fees:** **$310 total** — $250 solicitor admin + $25 SLED verification + $35 clerk of court, separate money orders ⚠️ confirm current; exemptions for the no-plea-deal dismissal cases; nonrefundable if denied at SLED stage (the $35 comes back if solicitor rejects ⚠️ verify per the 14th circuit's description).

**Personas:** (1) shoplifting conviction (30-day max) 2019, clean → § 910 eligible. (2) YOA burglary conviction 2016, done 2018, clean → § 920 eligible, once-ever. (3) DUI → never; pardon. (4) magistrate-court dismissal 2015 → should already be auto-expunged — check. (5) first-offense simple possession 2021 → eligible 2024+ → yes.

**Verify:** sccourts.org expungement FAQ (PDF is current and good) · § 17-22-910/-940/-950 + the § 22-5-910/920/930 texts · her test circuit's solicitor application page · H.3730 status.

# 4. ALABAMA — broad-ish law, brutal fee

**Terminology:** expungement, Ala. Code § 15-27 (2014 law + 2021 REDEEMER Act). Circuit court of the county where charges were filed. No automatic anything.

**Non-convictions:** dismissed w/ prejudice, no-bill, acquittal, nolle prossed w/o conditions → petition after **90 days**; dismissed w/o prejudice → after SOL expires or no-refile confirmation; diversion/drug-court/mental-health-court/veterans-court completions → **1 yr** post-completion. Unlimited expungements for non-convictions.

**Misdemeanor convictions (REDEEMER, eff. Jul 1, 2021):** **3 yrs** from conviction, all supervision done, everything paid; NOT violent, sex, moral-turpitude, or serious-traffic offenses — **DUI counts as serious traffic (explicitly as of Jul 1, 2023)**. Lifetime cap ⚠️: § 15-27-2.1 limits (secondary sources say 2 misdemeanor-conviction expungements; verify the section text).

**Felony convictions:** only after a **full pardon with restoration of civil and political rights** from the Board of Pardons and Paroles, **plus 180 days** from the certificate; not violent/sex/moral-turpitude/serious-traffic; 1 pardoned-felony expungement lifetime. (Reclassified-felony exception: Act 2015-185 offenses w/ 15-yr clean record.)

**Fees — the headline:** **$500 administrative filing fee per case/arrest event** (§ 15-27-4; raised from $300; Act 2024-407 effective Oct 1, 2024 ⚠️ confirm current amount) + court costs. The highest flat fee in the country alongside LA. Indigency relief exists in § 15-27-4 ⚠️ verify mechanics — this is the AL call's main question. One fee covers multiple charges from the same arrest.

**Personas:** (1) charges no-billed 2023 → eligible now (fee!). (2) misdemeanor theft conviction 2020, paid → REDEEMER-eligible 2023+ → yes, $500. (3) DUI → never. (4) nonviolent felony 2010, clean → pardon path → then 180 days → expungement. (5) drug-court completion 2024 → eligible mid-2025.

**Verify:** § 15-27-1/-2/-2.1/-4 current text · montgomeryvlp.org CLE handout (practitioner-grade) · Board of Pardons and Paroles pardon application process · one circuit clerk on the $500 + indigency.

# 5. LOUISIANA — highest fees, newest automation

**Terminology:** motion to expunge (CCrP arts. 971–995) — records removed from public access, not destroyed; eligible = **mandatory grant**. Filed in the parish of arrest/conviction; standardized statewide forms (Louisiana Clerks of Court Association / art. 992 forms).

**Non-convictions:** acquittal, dismissal, refusal to prosecute → expungable; if no prosecution, wait out the charging time limits (felony-hard-labor 6 yrs / other felony 4 / misdemeanor 2 / fine-only 6 mo). Art. 893/894 set-aside-and-dismiss → immediate expungement path (LA's PBJ-equivalent — needs its own branch).

**Convictions — "cleansing periods" from sentence/probation/parole completion:**
- Misdemeanors (art. 977): **5 yrs** conviction-free; one misdemeanor expungement per 5-yr period; DWI: one per 10 yrs (+$50 OMV fee)
- Felonies (art. 978): **10 yrs** conviction-free (w/ DA certification of clean record); ⚠️ count conflict: some guides say one felony per lifetime, but current art. 978(F) says a person may expunge **more than one felony in a 10-yr period if each is eligible** (the old 15-yr/one-shot limits were repealed 2020) — encode from the article text, not the guides. First-offender pardon (automatic constitutional pardon) → expungement path if not violent/sex.
- Art. 978(E) surprise: six specific violent offenses (aggravated battery, second-degree battery, aggravated criminal damage, simple robbery, purse snatching, illegal use of weapons) ARE expungable after 10 yrs via contradictory hearing.
- Excluded: crimes of violence generally (R.S. 14:2(B)), sex-registry offenses, crimes against minors, domestic abuse battery, certain CDS.
- Unique plus: **court debt cannot block eligibility** — LA is the only state; say so.

**Fees:** capped **$550** ($250 LSP BCII + $50 sheriff + $50 DA + up to $200 clerk), nonrefundable, one fee per arrest event; first-offense marijuana possession reduced to **$300 until Aug 1, 2026** (sunset two weeks after your demo — cite it with the date); DA-certified fee waiver only for non-conviction outcomes with zero felony history; expedited (17-yr-old arrestee, 2025) and trafficking-victim paths fee-exempt.

**⚠️ THE AUTOMATED SYSTEM — the state's biggest story:** 2023 SB 111 directed LSP BCII to build an automated/simplified expungement request process for all art. 976/977/978-eligible records back to Jan 1, 2006, live **Jan 1, 2025**: submit basic info → Bureau expunges eligible records within 30 days, free. No damages remedy if records are missed. **Verify operational reality** — is the online portal live, what's it called, is it working? If yes, LA's tree opens with "try the free automated request before paying $550" — an enormous, verified money-saving answer.

**Legal aid:** Justice & Accountability Center of Louisiana — (504) 273-1091, workshops + the CLEAN JACKET eligibility app; louisianalawhelp.org; LSP Right to Review fingerprint check recommended pre-filing.

**Personas:** (1) misdemeanor conviction 2018, clean → art. 977 eligible — try automated path first. (2) felony possession 2012, clean → art. 978 eligible. (3) simple robbery 2010, clean → 978(E) contradictory-hearing path — the surprise-yes. (4) domestic abuse battery → excluded. (5) first-offense marijuana possession → $300 window until Aug 1, 2026 — dated urgency copy.

**Verify:** arts. 977/978/983 current text (legis.la.gov) · lsp.org BCII expungement page (packet requirements + automated-system status) · JAC + louisianalawhelp.org · one parish clerk on the clerk-fee portion.
