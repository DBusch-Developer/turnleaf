Standing rules apply. Eleven states this wave — entries are tighter than Waves 1–5 because most of these regimes are simpler, but every claim below traces to a statute or official page found today, with ⚠️ where sources conflict or a call is needed. # 1. KENTUCKY — everything runs through the certificate

**Terminology:** expungement. Four statutes: § 431.073 (Class D felonies, vacate-and-expunge), § 431.078 (misdemeanors), § 431.076 (acquittals/dismissals), § 431.079 (the certification gate). Plus void-and-seal for drug offenses (§§ 218A.275(8), 218A.276).

**The gate:** a **Certificate of Eligibility** from KSP/AOC is required before filing under .073/.076/.078 — $40, valid only 30 days once issued, and **KSP's own page says processing averages 4–5 months.** That backlog is the dominant practical fact of Kentucky expungement; encode "start the certificate first, plan around the wait."

**Non-convictions (§ 431.076):** acquittals and dismissals-with-prejudice after Jul 15, 2020 → **automatic**, 30 days after the case ends (not plea-deal dismissals). Older cases and lower traffic → petition, 60-day wait, **no filing fee** (DPA-confirmed). Felony charges never indicted → expungable.

**Misdemeanors (§ 431.078):** most misdemeanors/violations, **5 yrs** from sentence or probation completion; **$100 filing fee** (in the statute text); excludes sex offenses and offenses against children ⚠️ full exclusion list from statute.

**Class D felonies (§ 431.073):** ~61 enumerated felonies + four pathways (enumerated list, same-incident sweep, pardoned felonies, multiple-D-felony provision); **5 yrs** after sentence/probation/parole completion; no convictions in the prior 5 yrs, none pending; **$50 filing + $250 on grant, payable over 18 months** (expungement isn't final until paid); ⚠️ 2023 amendment (eff. Jun 29, 2023) allows expunging **multiple** qualifying felonies — older guides say once-per-lifetime; encode from the amended statute. Class A/B/C never (except pardoned).

**Watch:** SB 290 (automatic expungement) **failed in the 2026 session** — no automation.
**Sources/orgs:** kycourts.gov expungement pages (official), DPA Expungement Guide (May 2024, practitioner-grade), expungeky.com FAQ, Louisville Goodwill/Urban League fee-help clinics.

**Personas:** (1) dismissed-with-prejudice 2022 → should be auto-expunged — check. (2) misdemeanor theft 2018, done → eligible, $100+$40. (3) Class D possession 2017, clean → eligible, ~$340 all-in, 4–5 month cert wait first. (4) two Class D felonies, separate incidents → 2023 amendment ⚠️ — the verify-then-encode branch. (5) Class C felony → pardon only.

**Verify:** § 431.073/.076/.078/.079 current text · kycourts.gov certification process page · KSP expungement page (backlog language) · DPA guide · one circuit clerk on the fee mechanics.

# 2. OREGON — free to file, slow to finish

**Terminology:** set aside (ORS 137.225) = sealing. SB 397 (eff. Jan 1, 2022) was the overhaul; a **2025 amendment (ch. 395)** added that expired money-judgment obligations count as sentence-complete — unpaid old LFOs no longer block.

**Waits (from conviction or release, whichever later; no other convictions during the wait; supervision done):** Class B felony **7 yrs** (non-person, non-firearms) · Class C felony **5 yrs** · Class A misdemeanor **3 yrs** · Class B/C misdemeanor, violation, contempt **1 yr**. Class A felonies excluded (marijuana exceptions); person felonies, most sex offenses, child/elder-victim offenses, and **traffic offenses including DUII** excluded — DUII diversion dismissals also NOT expungable (unique trap).

**Non-convictions:** no-charge arrests → 60 days; acquittals/dismissals → anytime per § (1)(c) — BUT ⚠️ a known drafting error (old § (9) cross-reference) made some dismissed charges wait conviction-length periods; practitioner article flagged it unfixed as of 2024 — verify current statute text and county practice.

**Fees:** **court filing fee eliminated** by SB 397; OSP record-check/fingerprint fee remains — ⚠️ sources conflict: $33 (Powell Law) vs $80 (fingerprint-card provisions/others). One OSP fee covers multi-county filings. Marijuana convictions: ORS 475C.397 — anytime, **zero fees, no fingerprints**.

**Process reality:** DA has 120 days to object; "shall grant" unless clear-and-convincing public-safety findings; **county backlogs run up to ~2 years** (practitioner-documented) — the honest timeline sentence. Small counties misapply the law on self-filed petitions; partial expungements confuse clerks.

**Orgs:** Legal Aid Services of Oregon 1-800-351-7248; courts.oregon.gov forms.

**Personas:** (1) B felony drug delivery 2015, clean → eligible since 2022 (was 20-yr wait before SB 397 — many people don't know they became eligible). (2) A misdemeanor 2021 → eligible 2024. (3) DUII diversion completed → NOT expungable — expectation-setter. (4) dismissed theft charge → the drafting-error ⚠️ branch. (5) old unpaid fines, judgment expired → 2025 amendment says eligible now.

**Verify:** ORS 137.225 current text (incl. the 2025 c.395 note + the dismissal subsection) · OSP fee amount · one circuit court clerk (Multnomah) on forms + current backlog · 475C.397.

# 3. IOWA — non-convictions plus exactly one misdemeanor, ever

**Terminology:** expungement (confidential, not destroyed). Chapter 901C. **No felony conviction expungement exists — felonies → pardon only.**

**Non-convictions (§ 901C.2):** all charges in the case acquitted/dismissed + **all court costs PAID** (an LFO gate on non-convictions — unusual and harsh; flag in UI) + 180 days passed (waivable for good cause, e.g. identity theft); not deferred-judgment dismissals (those use 907.9); not insanity/incompetency dismissals.

**Deferred judgments (§ 907.9):** completed deferred → **automatic** expungement (post-Jul-2013 completions; earlier ones and rural unsupervised-probation cases may need a motion) — check-your-record branch.

**Misdemeanor convictions (§ 901C.3, since 2019):** **8 yrs** from conviction; all LFOs paid; no pending charges; not previously granted two deferred judgments; ~25 excluded categories (OWI § 321J.2, assault variants, harassment, stalking, weapons ch. 724, sex offenses, etc.); **ONCE per lifetime** — one application, multiple misdemeanors only if same transaction. Iowa Legal Aid flags the strategic problem: people with several convictions must choose which one, forever. Turnleaf's IA tree needs the "eligible — but is this the one you want to spend it on?" branch, cousin to Indiana's.

**Fees:** ⚠️ no statutory filing fee found for 901C petitions — verify with a clerk (application filed in the criminal case).

**Personas:** (1) dismissed case 2020, costs paid → eligible. (2) deferred judgment done 2016 → should already be expunged — check. (3) misdemeanor theft 2015 → 8-yr wait met 2023 → eligible, once-ever decision. (4) OWI → never; honest-no. (5) felony conviction → pardon only.

**Verify:** §§ 901C.2/.3 + 907.9 text (legis.iowa.gov PDFs are current) · Iowa Legal Aid guide · Polk County clerk on fee/form.

# 4. NEVADA — sealing tiers plus the package rule

**Terminology:** record sealing (NRS 179.245 convictions, 179.255 non-convictions). No automation (Legislature declared pro-sealing policy in 179.2405 but built no machine).

**Waits (from release/discharge — and completion includes fines per practitioner sources ⚠️ verify clock start):** Cat A felony / crime of violence / residential burglary **10 yrs** · Cat B/C/D felonies **5 yrs** · Cat E felony **2 yrs** · gross misdemeanor **2 yrs** · misdemeanor DV battery, misdemeanor DUI, welfare fraud **7 yrs** · misdemeanor battery/harassment/stalking/protection-order violation **2 yrs** · other misdemeanors **1 yr** ⚠️ confirm the 1-yr general misdemeanor tier from statute. Never: crimes against children (179D.0357), sex offenses, **felony DUI**, home invasion w/ deadly weapon.

**The package rule (big UX fact):** Nevada seals records as a complete set — one still-ineligible case blocks everything; a new conviction resets. Encode as its own gate question.

**Non-convictions (179.255):** dismissals/acquittals → **immediately**, no wait (commonly misunderstood); declined-prosecution → SOL run, or 8 yrs from arrest, or DA agreement. Decriminalized offenses (marijuana ≤2.5 oz) → immediate (AB 192 2019). Pardoned convictions → sealed on receipt of pardon (2021).

**Presumption:** rebuttable presumption FOR sealing if requirements met (179.2445; not if dishonorably discharged from probation); prosecutor stipulation → court **shall** seal; 30-day objection window; hearings often avoidable.

**Costs/process:** no single statutory fee — SCOPE reports from each arresting agency + criminal history + certified copies; **~$150 all-in self-filed in Las Vegas Justice Court** (practitioner figure ⚠️ verify); Carson City Repository months-long backlog to actually seal after the order. Nevada Legal Services Record Sealing Manual (2024) = the self-help authority; note sealing restores vote/office/jury but NOT firearms (pardon only).

**Personas:** (1) Cat E possession 2021, discharged → 2-yr wait met → eligible. (2) dismissal 2024 → seal NOW. (3) misdemeanor DUI 2020 → 7-yr wait → 2027. (4) eligible 2016 gross misd + brand-new 2025 misdemeanor → package rule blocks everything — the teaching persona. (5) felony DUI → never.

**Verify:** NRS 179.245/.255/.2445 text · NLS manual (current edition) · LV Justice Court / Eighth Judicial District sealing pages + fee reality · Repository processing time.

# 5. ARKANSAS — the quiet overachiever

**Terminology:** sealing (Comprehensive Criminal Record Sealing Act of 2013, § 16-90-1401 et seq.); uniform petition/order forms statewide (ACIC). **No filing fee since July 2019 (Act 680)** — Legal Aid of Arkansas confirms.

**Misdemeanors (§ 16-90-1405):** most sealable **immediately** on sentence completion (completion includes LFOs paid, § 16-90-1404(1)); unlimited count; 5-yr wait for a short serious list (negligent homicide A-misd, battery 3rd, indecent exposure, DV-adjacent, etc. ⚠️ list from statute); **misdemeanor DWI: 10-yr wait** (per Legal Aid ⚠️ confirm — a surprisingly long outlier).

**Felonies (§ 16-90-1406):** non-violent Class C/D + Class A/B **drug** felonies → sealable **immediately** on completion; violent C/D → +5 yrs; max ONE prior felony conviction (same-episode felonies count as one). Ineligible (§ 1408): Y/A/B felonies (non-drug), manslaughter, sex offenses, violent felonies, unclassified >10-yr max, CDL-holder traffic felonies.

**Process:** file in the court of the case; serve PA within 3 days; misdemeanor objection window 30 days; felony: court must wait **90 days** before granting; standards (§ 1415): misdemeanors = shall seal absent clear-and-convincing reasons; felonies = discretionary. Re-petition after denial: 90 days (misd) / 1 yr. Non-convictions (§§ 1409/1410): arrests, nolle, dismissals, acquittals sealable, no wait ⚠️ confirm. First Offender Act 346 + pardoned convictions (§ 1411) have their own doors. The 2019 amendments explicitly announced intent to move toward automation — none exists yet.

**Personas:** (1) Class D theft felony, sentence done last month → eligible NOW, free — the state's headline. (2) misdemeanor 2024, fines paid → immediate. (3) two separate felony convictions → one-prior-felony cap ⚠️ analysis branch. (4) DWI misdemeanor 2018 → 10-yr wait → 2028. (5) Class B drug felony, done 2023 → immediate — the surprise-yes.

**Verify:** §§ 16-90-1405/1406/1408/1413/1415 text · ACIC uniform forms page · Legal Aid of Arkansas sealing guides · one circuit clerk (Pulaski) confirming zero fee.

# 6. MISSISSIPPI — one felony, ever, with a hearing

**Terminology:** expunction (§ 99-19-71). Circuit/justice/county/municipal courts.

**Misdemeanors:** first-offender misdemeanor (non-traffic) → petition the convicting court, **no statutory wait** ⚠️ confirm; municipal/justice-court discretionary expungement of any misdemeanors after 2 yrs good conduct; under-23 first-offender path.

**Felonies (post-Jul 2019 general rule):** **ONE felony expunction per lifetime**, **5 yrs** after successful completion of all sentence terms, all fines/costs paid; discretionary — court must find "rehabilitated," 10-day DA notice, hearing; "one conviction" = common nucleus of operative facts. Excluded: crimes of violence (§ 97-3-2), 1st-degree arson, drug trafficking, 3rd+ DUI, felon-in-possession, sex-registration failure, embezzlement ⚠️ + more from statute.

**Non-convictions:** court **shall** expunge arrests released w/o charge, dismissals, dropped charges, no-disposition, not-guilty — on petition (not automatic).

**Fees:** **$150 statutory filing fee** (§ 99-19-72: $100 judicial fund + $40 DA fund + $10 clerk) ⚠️ confirm current + whether it applies to non-conviction petitions.

**Watch:** automatic-expungement bills introduced 2024 (HB 801), 2025 (HB 1117), 2026 (HB 1344) — the pattern says none has passed; ⚠️ confirm the 2026 bill died before encoding "no automation."

**Personas:** (1) felony possession 2018, done, paid → one-shot eligible — spend-it-wisely branch (MS joins IN/IA in the "don't file casually" club). (2) first misdemeanor 2023 → eligible now. (3) embezzlement felony → excluded — the surprise-no. (4) dismissed charge → petition, shall-grant. (5) second felony after a prior expunction → never.

**Verify:** § 99-19-71 current text + § 99-19-72 fee · a circuit clerk (Hinds) · Mississippi Center for Justice expungement resources · 2026 HB 1344 outcome.

# 7. KANSAS — discretionary but broad, with a firearm twist

**Terminology:** expungement (K.S.A. 21-6614) = sealing (terms used interchangeably; record survives for listed agencies).

**Waits (from sentence satisfied / discharge from supervision):** **3 yrs** — traffic/tobacco infractions, misdemeanors, old class D/E felonies, nongrid + severity 6–10 nondrug felonies, drug-grid level 4 (pre-2012) / level 5 (post-2012); **5 yrs** — remaining eligible (more serious) felonies + first DUI; 2nd+ DUI 7–10 yrs; diversion agreements 3 yrs; **specialty-court (drug/veterans court) graduates → petition immediately, docket fee waivable.** Never: murder, manslaughter, rape, sex offenses vs. minors, child abuse, commercial DUI, and anyone still required to register.

**Standard (§ (h)):** court SHALL expunge if: no felony conviction in past 2 yrs + none pending, circumstances warrant, consistent with public welfare (+ firearm-safety finding for felonies). **Expungement restores firearm rights** (§ (k)(2), 2021) — rare among states; worth a sentence.

**Fees:** ⚠️ conflict — statute text says **$176 docket fee**; current guides + Judicial Council materials say **$195** (set by Supreme Court order, which updates) — phone target. Waived for non-convictions; poverty affidavit available. Arrest records: separate § 22-2410.

**Process:** hearing ~60+ days out; KBI takes 8–12 weeks post-grant; total 2–4 months. **Kansas Legal Services runs free expungement clinics — kls_expunge@klsinc.org.** KBI fact sheet (Feb 2025) = official overview.

**Personas:** (1) severity-8 theft felony 2019, discharged 2021 → 3-yr wait met → eligible. (2) first DUI 2018, done 2019 → 5-yr wait met → eligible (DUI expungable here, unlike most of this wave!). (3) drug-court graduate last month → petition NOW, fee waivable. (4) registrable offense → not while registered. (5) diversion completed 2021 → eligible.

**Verify:** 21-6614 current text (ksrevisor.gov) · the docket fee ($176 vs $195) with a district clerk · KBI fact sheet · KLS clinic details.

# 8. NEW MEXICO — degree-laddered waits, statute-first encoding

**Terminology:** expungement (Criminal Record Expungement Act, § 29-3A, eff. Jan 1, 2020 — one of the nation's broader laws when passed).

**Non-convictions (§ 29-3A-4):** **1 yr** from final disposition — acquittals, dismissals, nolle, pre-prosecution diversion, conditional discharge; 2021 amendments added MV penalty assessments, allowed one petition to cover multiple records in a district, and require the petition be filed under seal with a DPS RAP sheet ≤90 days old attached.

**Convictions (§ 29-3A-5) — conviction-free waits from last sentence completed (any jurisdiction), fines/fees paid, restitution fulfilled, justice-served balancing:**
- Municipal ordinance / most misdemeanors: **2 yrs**
- Misdemeanor aggravated battery + 4th-degree felonies: **4 yrs**
- 3rd-degree felonies: **6 yrs**
- 2nd-degree felonies: **8 yrs**
- 1st-degree felonies + anything under the Crimes Against Household Members Act (DV): **10 yrs**
Excluded entirely: crimes against children, offenses causing great bodily harm/death, sex offenses, **embezzlement** (the odd one out — flag it), and **DWI** (even first-offense deferred).
⚠️ Discrepancy material: legal-blog sources flatten this to "2 yrs misdemeanors / 4 yrs felonies" — wrong; encode the full ladder from statute.

**Cannabis (§ 29-3A-8, 2021 + 2023 HB 314):** possession ≤2 oz → **automatic** expungement 2 yrs after conviction/arrest ⚠️ verify operational status — NM's only automation.

**Fees:** ⚠️ no statutory fee in CREA; district court civil filing fee applies (~$132 historically) — phone target. Forms + the official Supreme Court handout on nmcourts.gov.

**Personas:** (1) 4th-degree felony possession 2019, done 2020 → 4-yr wait met → eligible. (2) dismissal 2024 → eligible 2025. (3) DWI → never — honest-no. (4) cannabis possession 2019 → should be auto-expunged — check. (5) 2nd-degree felony 2014, done 2016 → eligible 2024.

**Verify:** §§ 29-3A-4/-5/-8 current text · nmcourts.gov expungement forms + Supreme Court handout · district court filing fee · cannabis automation status (call DPS or Second Judicial).

# 9. NEBRASKA — the last set-aside-only state

**The headline (CCRC's 50-state comparison says it flatly):** Nebraska is **the only state whose conviction relief is limited to set-aside, with no general sealing authority.** A granted set-aside nullifies the conviction and removes civil disabilities — but the conviction **stays on the public record**, annotated "set aside." Encode that sentence verbatim-honest; Nebraskans searching "expungement" need the expectation reset in the first breath.

**Set-aside (§ 29-2264):** eligible if sentenced to probation, fine-only, or community service (on completion + payment), or — since LB 881 (2020) — imprisonment **≤1 yr** (completed, nothing pending, not registrable, not vehicular homicide, no denial in past 2 yrs). Discretionary (Brunsen factors). Sentences over a year → **pardon only.** No fee typically; nebraskajudicial.gov self-help has the forms and a plainspoken guide. Firearms NOT restored (pardon board only; county practice split on DV misdemeanors — live litigation).

**What CAN come off:** non-convictions (§ 29-3523 — arrest w/o charge on timelines, dismissed cases, erroneous arrests by clear-and-convincing showing); **pardoned convictions → sealable** (2021); trafficking-survivor convictions → set-aside + sealing (§ 29-3005). Voting: restored automatically on sentence completion (2024 LB20 killed the 2-yr wait) — useful adjacent fact.

**Orgs:** Legal Aid of Nebraska **Clean Slate Program** + AccessLine.

**Personas:** (1) felony probation completed → set-aside eligible; record stays visible — the honest-yes-but. (2) 3-yr prison sentence → pardon only. (3) dismissed charge → § 29-3523 relief. (4) pardoned conviction → NOW sealable. (5) misdemeanor fine-only 2020, paid → set-aside now.

**Verify:** § 29-2264 + § 29-3523 text · nebraskajudicial.gov set-aside self-help pages · Legal Aid NE Clean Slate · Board of Pardons process basics.

# 10. IDAHO — honest-limited, with a 2023 door most guides missed

**Terminology:** three narrow tools, none called "expungement of convictions":
1. **Non-conviction expungement (§ 67-3004(10)):** no-charge after 1 yr / acquitted / all charges dismissed → **written request to Idaho State Police BCI** (administrative — not a court petition; ISP has the application PDF) → fingerprints + criminal history expunged, court file sealed. Excludes § 19-2604(1) dismissals (withheld-judgment dismissals don't qualify for sealing).
2. **Shielding (§ 67-3004(11), HB 149, 2023) — the new door:** ONE conviction — non-violent misdemeanor OR felony drug possession — petition after **5 conviction-free yrs** from full sentence completion (probation, parole, fines, restitution); "held accountable" standard; record hidden from public, deniable; law enforcement retains access; assaultive/violent misdemeanors excluded. ⚠️ Most older guides say Idaho has no conviction relief — this is fresh-law Discrepancy material.
3. **Withheld judgment (§ 19-2601, ONE per lifetime) → § 19-2604 dismissal:** plead guilty, judgment withheld, complete probation → dismissal, rights restored (incl. firearms per case law), record reads "Dismissed by Court" — but NOT sealed. Also: retroactive felony-to-misdemeanor reduction under 19-2604(2).

**No general conviction expungement otherwise; no automation.** Trafficking-survivor vacatur exists.

**Fees:** ISP administrative request appears free (documentation only ⚠️ confirm); shielding petition = court filing ⚠️ phone target.

**Personas:** (1) arrested 2023, never charged → ISP request now, free. (2) misdemeanor possession 2018, everything paid 2019 → shielding eligible 2024 — the 2023-law persona. (3) violent misdemeanor → excluded; honest-no. (4) withheld judgment completed → move for dismissal; explain it's visible-but-dismissed. (5) old felony theft → no path but pardon/commutation — honest-no.

**Verify:** § 67-3004(10)/(11) current text · ISP BCI expungement application + shielding practice · one district clerk (Ada) on shielding filings · courts' self-help.

# 11. WEST VIRGINIA — clean tiers, an acceleration lane, and a once-ever rule

**Terminology:** expungement (§ 61-11-26 convictions; § 61-11-25 non-convictions). Circuit court of conviction.

**Waits:** single misdemeanor **1 yr** · multiple misdemeanors **2 yrs** from the last · non-violent felony (or several from the same transaction) **5 yrs** — each from conviction/incarceration/supervision completion, whichever is latest.
**Acceleration (§ 61-11-26a):** complete an approved substance-abuse treatment/recovery program OR a WV Dept. of Ed job-readiness course → waits drop to **90 days / 1 yr / 3 yrs**, AND the $100 WSP fee is waived. A treatment-and-work-based fast lane is unique in the country — Jobs & Hope WV is the program hub; deck-worthy.

**⚠️ The once-ever rule:** the Supreme Court's official petition instructions (SCA-C900) state a person may request expungement under §§ 26/26a **only once**. Confirm scope (once per person? per statute?) — it changes strategy the way Indiana's one-petition rule does.

**Exclusions:** violent felonies, felonies with minor victims, sexual offenses, deadly-weapon offenses, DV assault/battery, DUI, driving-suspended, CDL offenses ⚠️ full (c) list; but an old DUI (5+ yrs) doesn't block expunging an unrelated eligible felony.

**Non-convictions (§ 61-11-25):** acquittals + dismissals → petition **60 days** after; completed pretrial diversion / deferred adjudication dismissals → expungable (DV-related carve-outs).

**Fees/process:** **$100 to WSP records division** (§ 26(n); waived on the 26a fast lane) + circuit court filing fee ⚠️ amount per county — phone target; serve WSP superintendent, PA, police chief, warden; agencies certify compliance within 60 days.

**Watch:** ⚠️ check whether any 2024–26 automation bill moved (HB 4344-era proposals) — encode "petition-only" unless a call says otherwise.

**Personas:** (1) single misdemeanor 2023, done → 1-yr wait met → eligible. (2) NV felony 2020 + completed recovery program → 26a: eligible at 3 yrs (2023+), WSP fee waived — the fast-lane persona. (3) DV battery → excluded. (4) acquitted → 60 days → expunge. (5) already used expungement once → the ⚠️ once-ever branch.

**Verify:** §§ 61-11-25/-26/-26a text (code.wvlegislature.gov) · SCA-C900 instructions (once-ever language) · circuit clerk (Kanawha) filing fee · Jobs & Hope WV / Legal Aid of WV.

## Cross-wave flags

1. **Three states join the "spend it wisely" club:** Iowa (one misdemeanor expungement per lifetime), Mississippi (one felony ever), West Virginia (once-ever per the official instructions ⚠️). With Indiana, that's a four-state UX pattern: eligibility isn't the question — timing is.
2. **Arkansas and Kansas are the wave's good-news stories:** AR = free, immediate for C/D felonies; KS = DUI actually expungable + firearm restoration. Neither is widely known.
3. **Nebraska's page is one honest paragraph** and it must not pretend otherwise. With Wisconsin, you now have both flavors of honest-no: "at sentencing or never" (WI) and "set aside but never hidden" (NE).
4. **Idaho's 2023 shielding law is the wave's freshest-law flag** — competitor tools built on pre-2023 data will say Idaho has nothing.
5. **Kentucky's real product insight is operational, not legal:** the 4–5 month certificate backlog means "start now" is the advice even for people whose wait ends next year.
