# 1. HAWAII — administrative, cheap, and mostly non-convictions

**Structure (unusual):** expungement is ADMINISTRATIVE — apply to the **Attorney General's Hawaii Criminal Justice Data Center (HCJDC)**, not a court (HRS § 831-3.2). "Shall issue" for arrests/charges without conviction. Exclusions: bail-forfeiture non-convictions (felony/misd: never; petty misd/violation: 5-yr wait), absconders, ch. 704 mental-disease acquittals/706-607 hospitalizations.

**Deferred pleas (DAG/DANC, ch. 853):** expungable **1 yr** after discharge and dismissal; prostitution (712-1200) deferrals: ⚠️ the application PDF says 3 yrs, HCJDC's current page says 4 — resolve by phone (the kind of one-digit discrepancy this project lives for).

**Convictions:** court order FIRST, then HCJDC application — only for: under-21 DUI (§ 291E-64(e)), first-time drug offenders (§§ 706-622.5/.8), first-time property offenders (§ 706-622.9). Everything else: no path. **Pardoned convictions do NOT qualify — they stay on the record with a pardon notation** (counterintuitive; encode it).

**Fees/process:** **$35 first-time / $50 repeat** (incl. $10 nonrefundable), cashier's check/money order only; **120 days, no expediting**; status not given by phone/email (confidentiality) — applicants notified by mail only if denied.

**Fresh law:** **Act 003 (2025), eff. Jul 1, 2025** — HCJDC now auto-transmits expungement orders to the Judiciary for court-record sealing on eCourt Kokua (pre-Jul-2025 certificates still require a separate request to the court; sealing can be denied if co-defendants or non-expunged charges share the case). Two-system honesty: HCJDC clears the state criminal history; the court record is a second step.

**Contact (from HCJDC's own page):** Expungement Section **(808) 587-3348**, 465 S. King St. Rm 102, Honolulu.

**Personas:** (1) arrested, charges dropped 2023 → apply now, $35, 120 days. (2) DANC plea, dismissed 2024 → eligible 2025. (3) misdemeanor conviction (ordinary) → no path; honest-no + pardon note. (4) first-time drug offender sentenced under 706-622.5 → court order → HCJDC. (5) expunged in 2023 but still on eCourt Kokua → the pre-Act-003 sealing-request branch.

**Verify:** § 831-3.2 current text · ag.hawaii.gov/hcjdc expungement + FAQ pages · the 3-vs-4-yr prostitution-deferral conflict · Act 003 auto-transmit working in practice.

# 2. NEW HAMPSHIRE — annulment, with a trap for early filers

**Terminology:** annulment (RSA 651:5). Petition the court of disposition; separate petition per charge; **$125 court filing fee per court location** (official checklist) + **$100 State Police record-correction fee on grant** (waived for non-convictions) + ~$25 record copy; DOC investigation (questionnaire + fee) for most conviction annulments; 3–6 months typical. ⚠️ Some guides say $100 court fee — the Judicial Branch checklist says $125; confirm.

**Waits (from completion of ALL sentence terms incl. LFOs):** violation **1 yr** · Class B misdemeanor ⚠️ (statute historically 3 yrs; some current summaries say 1 — encode from the current RSA 651:5(III) text) · Class A misdemeanor **3 yrs** · Class B felony **5 yrs** · Class A felony **10 yrs** · sexual assault (632-A:4) and felony indecent exposure **10 yrs** · **DV misdemeanor 10 yrs** (with stacking if a new DV conviction lands) · **drug felonies under RSA 318-B:26, II: 2 yrs** (shorter than the speeding-ticket rule below — genuinely odd) · MV offenses that are habitual-offender predicates: **7 yrs** even for violations (the CLE materials relish this: a speeding ticket can wait longer than a drug felony).

**THE TRAP:** file before the waiting period has run → petition denied → **no new petition for 3 years** (RSA 651:5(IV)). Turnleaf's NH page needs a red-letter "do not file early" warning; this is the sharpest procedural landmine in all 50 states.

**Multi-conviction rule:** annulment barred until time requirements met for ALL offenses of record, and barred entirely if any conviction is in a never-eligible class — softened by *State v. Williams* (2020): petition the latest-occurring offense first and work backwards ⚠️ encode carefully.

**Never:** violent crimes (listed: murder, first-degree assault, AFSA/FSA, kidnapping, robbery, Class A arson, incest, CSAM felonies), felony obstruction of justice, extended-term sentences.

**Automatic:** dismissals/acquittals after Jan 1, 2019 → **auto-annulled 30 days** after disposition. Streamlined post-2019 violation/Class-B-misd process: 20-day prosecutor objection, no DOC investigation. Cannabis: ≤¾ oz pre-Sep-2017 → anytime (651:5-b); misdemeanor/violation cannabis pre-2025 → anytime even with unpaid fines (651:5-c).

**Personas:** (1) Class A misd 2020, done 2021 → eligible 2024. (2) B felony drug possession, done 2023 → the 2-yr drug wait → eligible 2025 — surprise-fast. (3) eligible in 8 months, eager → the DON'T-FILE-YET warning persona. (4) DV misdemeanor 2018 → 10-yr wait → 2028. (5) dismissal 2024 → already auto-annulled — check.

**Verify:** RSA 651:5 current text (gc.nh.gov) · courts.nh.gov annulment checklist (fees + forms NHJB-2202/3057) · the Class B misd wait · DOC fee amount.

# 3. MAINE — the age cap is gone; most guides haven't noticed

**Terminology:** Maine has **no expungement**; the remedy is a **post-judgment motion to seal** (15 M.R.S. ch. 310-A, §§ 2261–2265). Records classification (16 § 703) already makes most non-conviction information confidential by law — a structural quirk: Maine dismissals/acquittals are non-public without any petition.

**What's sealable (as amended 2024 — HP1435):** **ALL Class E convictions** (max 6 mo/$1,000) except sexual assault — **the old 18-to-27 age limitation was REMOVED in 2024** ⚠️ Discrepancy gold: most online guides (and both major court-records sites) still state the age cap; encode from the current statute. Also sealable: **Class D and E marijuana convictions from before Jan 30, 2017.** Class A–D crimes otherwise: no sealing — pardon only.

**Prerequisites (§ 2262):** **4 yrs** since sentence satisfied (incl. LFOs); no other convictions; no deferred-disposition dismissals in the window. Motion CR-218; filing ~**$5** (cheapest in the nation ⚠️ confirm current); sealed = deniable except to criminal-justice agencies and listed entities.

**Fresh law:** **LD 1871 (enacted Jan 11, 2026):** sealing for ANY conviction substantially resulting from sex trafficking/sexual exploitation — anytime, no waiting period, documentation creates a presumption. Two weeks old in legislative terms; competitors won't have it.

**Pardons:** 5 yrs post-sentence; Board won't take OUI, registry-removal, or firearms-motivated applications; full-and-free pardon → record becomes confidential.

**Personas:** (1) Class E theft 2019 at age 45, done 2020 → **eligible 2024 under the new law** — the age-cap-repeal persona. (2) Class D assault → no sealing; pardon path. (3) pre-2017 Class D marijuana → sealable. (4) dismissal → already confidential by classification — explain, don't file. (5) OUI → no sealing, no pardon — the double honest-no.

**Verify:** 15 M.R.S. §§ 2261/2262/2264 current text · courts.maine.gov sealing page + CR-218 · the $5 fee · LD 1871 effective status.

# 4. MONTANA — one shot, five years, and a renumbered statute

**⚠️ Citation catch (the TX/55A move again):** the 2019 Misdemeanor Expungement Clarification Act (HB 543) **repealed § 46-18-1101** and recodified into **§§ 46-18-1102 through -1111**. Sources (including the DOJ's own page and most attorneys) still cite 1101; Turnleaf should cite the live sections with a "recodified 2019" note.

**Misdemeanor expungement (district court; TRUE expungement — permanent destruction from the DOJ criminal-history system):** **presumed ("shall grant")** after **5 conviction-free yrs** since sentence completion (incl. LFOs + court-ordered treatment), nothing pending — OR immediately, no wait, for military applicants/enlistees blocked by the record (a unique branch). **NOT presumed** (discretionary, factor-balanced) for assault, partner/family-member assault, stalking, sexual assault, protective-order violations, and DUI — still possible, just uphill. Victim notification; prosecutor response.

**Once per lifetime** — one order, but it may cover multiple misdemeanors (court may grant all, some, or none per § 1110) ⚠️ practitioner-reported inconsistency between jurisdictions on bundling — a call question. Post-grant chore: petitioner mails order + FD-258 fingerprint card + DOJ form to CRISS in Helena.

**Felonies: no expungement at all** — deferred-imposition dismissal (if you got one at sentencing), or pardon (rare: a handful of recommendations a year). Marijuana convictions: separate legalization-era path with OCA resources. Non-convictions: 2019 HB 47 — no-charge releases and invalidated convictions trigger return/expungement of prints and photos via the state repository (semi-automatic).

**Fees:** district court filing fee ⚠️ amount — phone target; courts.mt.gov publishes a self-help forms packet; Montana Legal Services Association for free help.

**Personas:** (1) two misdemeanors 2017, done 2018, clean → presumed-eligible for the one lifetime shot — bundle both. (2) DUI misdemeanor → discretionary branch, not barred. (3) enlisting in the Guard, conviction blocking → immediate petition — the military persona. (4) felony → honest-no (deferred/pardon notes). (5) already used the lifetime shot → done forever.

**Verify:** §§ 46-18-1104/-1107/-1108/-1110 current text · dojmt.gov conviction-expungement page (CRISS mechanics) · courts.mt.gov packet · district court filing fee (Yellowstone or Missoula clerk).

# 5. RHODE ISLAND — first-offender core plus a multi-misdemeanor lane

**Terminology:** expungement (convictions; § 12-1.3) vs sealing (non-convictions; § 12-1-12). Discretionary — judge must find good moral character + rehabilitation even when timing is met.

**Paths (§ 12-1.3-2):**
- **First offender** (single conviction, nothing else ever): misdemeanor **5 yrs** / felony **10 yrs** from sentence completion; crimes of violence (long list incl. burglary) never; **all court LFOs paid** unless reduced/waived.
- **Multi-misdemeanor** (2017 reform): more than one but **fewer than six** misdemeanors, no felony → expunge any/all **10 yrs** after the last sentence; excludes DV (ch. 12-29), DUI, and chemical-test refusal (those remain individually expungable on the single-misdemeanor path if the person otherwise qualifies ⚠️ nuance from practitioners).
- **One first-offender felony only** — multiple felonies: never.
- Deferred sentences → expungement on completion (§ (e)); decriminalized offenses → anytime (§ (g)); marijuana auto-expungement (§ 12-1.3-5) ⚠️ status; prostitution/trafficking (11-34.1-5) 1 yr.

**Non-convictions:** sealing, free — and **Rule 48(a) dismissals on/after Jan 1, 2023 auto-seal** (§ 12-1-12.1(a)(1)); older dismissals petition.

**Fees/process:** **$100 fee payable on GRANT** (waivable for indigency); BCI record from the AG (4 Howard Ave, Cranston) **$5**; motion in the court of conviction; AG/police served; post-grant, deliver certified orders to BCI + arresting agency (the record isn't really gone until that chore is done — encode it).

**Personas:** (1) single misdemeanor 2018, done → 5-yr met → eligible (judge discretion caveat). (2) three misdemeanors, last sentence done 2015 → 10-yr multi path → eligible 2025. (3) single felony larceny 2012, clean → eligible 2022+; but burglary → never. (4) dismissal March 2023 → should be auto-sealed — check. (5) DUI misdemeanor + 2 others → DUI blocked from the multi path — the nuance persona.

**Verify:** § 12-1.3-1/-2/-3 current text (webserver.rilegislature.gov) · ripd.org resource guide · courts.ri.gov expungement info · the $100-on-grant mechanics with a District Court clerk.

# 6. SOUTH DAKOTA — arrests yes, convictions mostly no

**Expungement (§§ 23A-3-26 to -37) covers NON-CONVICTIONS only:** (1) **1 yr** after arrest with no charging instrument; (2) **1 yr** after formal dismissal of the whole case (or sooner on "compelling necessity," 2022); (3) **anytime** after acquittal. Discretionary "ends of justice" standard; prosecutor notice; hearing waivable if all parties consent (UJS-392). Official forms UJS-391/-394 + a Guide-and-File online interview (ujs.sd.gov) — friendly self-help for a restrictive state.

**Convictions:** no general path. The exceptions ARE the page:
- **Automatic removal (§ 23A-3-34):** petty offenses, municipal ordinance violations, and **Class 2 misdemeanors** (as the highest charge) come off the public record after conditions satisfied — ⚠️ sources split **5 vs 10 yrs**; encode from current statute text only. This is SD's quiet automation.
- **Suspended imposition of sentence (§ 23A-27-13):** first-offense discretionary at sentencing → sealed on successful completion (WI-adjacent: decided at sentencing, not after).
- **Diversion completions:** auto-expunged (2018, §§ 23A-3-35–37) — no motion needed.
- **Pardon → sealed**; exceptional pardon (24-14-8) 5-yr path; BCS record destruction at age 75 / misdemeanor records 10 yrs post-disposition (§ 23-6-8.1, director's discretion).
**DUI/motor-vehicle convictions: excluded entirely.**

**Costs:** DCI record **$24** (Pierre; (605) 773-3331 per their page); filing fee ⚠️ circuit clerk — phone.

**Personas:** (1) arrested 2023, never charged → eligible 2024. (2) Class 2 misdemeanor 2018, conditions done → possibly auto-removed already — check-record branch (and the 5-vs-10 ⚠️). (3) Class 1 misdemeanor conviction → honest-no (pardon note). (4) suspended imposition completed → sealed — confirm it happened. (5) felony → pardon only.

**Verify:** § 23A-3-27 and § 23A-3-34 current text (sdlegislature.gov) · ujs.sd.gov expungement self-help · the 5-vs-10 conflict · UJS clerk on filing fee.

# 7. NORTH DAKOTA — quietly one of the friendlier small states

**Sealing (ch. 12-60.1, 2019):** misdemeanors **3 yrs** / felonies **5 yrs** conviction-free from completion of incarceration/probation/parole; petition names arresting agency + prosecutor as respondents; discretionary factor-balancing (risk, rehabilitation, victim input); **NO FILING FEE — the statute forbids charging one** (put that on the fee-spectrum slide next to Alabama's $500). Ineligible: registrable sex offenders/offenders-against-children; violent/intimidation felonies must clear the 10-yr firearm-prohibition window. Denial → 3-yr re-petition bar. **DUI is sealable** (rare among this wave). Deferred imposition (§ 12.1-32-07.1): completion → set-aside + dismissal, **auto-sealed 61 days** after probation ends (misd/infractions, court rule).

**Fresh law:** **HB 1166 (2025):** non-conviction court records **auto-close 61 days** after a non-conviction order entered on/after **Aug 1, 2025**; older non-convictions → petition with a **mandatory 10-day grant** if requirements met (§ 12-60.1-05). Two weeks from operative at your demo — encode with the date.

**Also:** minor felonies reducible to misdemeanors; first-offender marijuana sealing; BCI record check for prep; ndcourts.gov petition instructions.

**Personas:** (1) misdemeanor 2020, probation done 2021 → 3-yr met → eligible, free. (2) C felony theft 2017, done 2019 → 5-yr met → eligible. (3) DUI misdemeanor 2021 → sealable 2024+ — the surprise-yes. (4) dismissal September 2025 → auto-closes in 61 days — wait, don't file. (5) old 2018 dismissal → petition, 10-day mandatory grant.

**Verify:** ch. 12-60.1 text (ndlegis.gov PDF confirms the no-fee line) · HB 1166 operative details · ndcourts.gov sealing instructions · BCI record process.

# 8. ALASKA — the deepest honest-no in the nation

**The rule:** **no expungement law exists.** Sealing (§ 12.62.180) only for records proven — beyond a reasonable doubt, to the agency head, administratively — to result from **mistaken identity or false accusation**. That's it. CCRC: with one 2024 marijuana exception, "Alaska law makes no provision for sealing or expunging records of a valid conviction."

**What Turnleaf CAN offer Alaskans:**
- **CourtView removal** (Admin Rule 40 / AS 22.35.030, Form TF-810): if the ENTIRE case ended in dismissal or acquittal, remove it from the public online index 60 days after disposition. Partial wins don't qualify — a dismissed charge in a case with any conviction stays up (encode plainly).
- **SIS set-aside (§ 12.55.085):** suspended imposition + completed probation → conviction set aside as a matter of right absent good cause — but per *Journey v. State*, the public record REMAINS, showing conviction + set-aside. Excluded: DUI/refusal, sex offenses, felony crimes against a person, firearm use, prior DV. Forward-looking (sentencing-stage), like Wisconsin.
- **Suspended entry of judgment (§ 12.55.078):** the newer tool — no conviction ever enters.
- **2024 marijuana law:** decriminalized possession convictions barred from release/publication ⚠️ verify scope/mechanics.
- **Pardons: functionally extinct — ~188 since statehood, none since 2006.** State that number; it's the honest context.

**Personas:** (1) entire case dismissed 2023 → TF-810 CourtView removal — the one real win. (2) misdemeanor conviction, probation done under SIS → set aside, still visible — expectation-setting. (3) any ordinary conviction → no path; pardon effectively unavailable. (4) mistaken-identity arrest → § 12.62.180 sealing. (5) old marijuana possession → the 2024 non-publication branch ⚠️.

**Verify:** § 12.62.180 + § 12.55.085 text · courts.alaska.gov TF-810 / Rule 40 · DPS record-sealing request form · the 2024 marijuana provision.

# 9. VERMONT — rewritten two weeks before your demo

**⚠️ THE fresh-law state of the fifty.** **Act 60 (2025), effective July 1, 2025**, restructured everything. Any source that predates it — meaning nearly every guide online — is wrong. Encode ONLY from the current statute (legislature.vermont.gov shows the post-Act-60 text) and the Judiciary's own page (vtcourts.gov/criminal/expungement documents the shift).

**The new architecture (13 V.S.A. ch. 230):**
- **Expungement** now limited to convictions for conduct **no longer a crime** — immediate on sentence completion + restitution/surcharges paid; record destroyed; "NO CRIMINAL RECORD EXISTS."
- **Sealing = the primary remedy:** qualifying misdemeanors (most, except listed violent crimes + sexual misconduct) → **3 yrs** from sentence completion (down from 5); qualifying felonies (non-violent, including burglary of unoccupied dwellings, listed property crimes, drug offenses **including trafficking**, and pardoned convictions) → **7 yrs**; misdemeanor DUI → **10 yrs** (excluding injury/death, school-bus, CDL).
- **Shall grant without a hearing** absent government objection; the "interests of justice" burden sits on the STATE to show sealing is contrary — a burden flip almost nowhere else has.
- The old no-new-convictions-during-the-wait requirement was **removed** (2025). Pending charge pauses the petition; denial → 2-yr re-file bar.
- **Ages 18–21 at offense:** petition after just **30 days** (replacing old auto-sealing-after-30-yrs).
- Non-convictions: § 7603 sealing.

**Fees:** **$90 per docket + $30 VCIC record check** (waivers available). **Vermont Legal Aid: 1-800-917-7787 / vtlawhelp.org/expungement** — free clinics.

**Personas:** (1) misdemeanor possession 2021, done 2022 → sealed 2025 under the NEW 3-yr rule (old law said 2027 — show the delta). (2) felony grand larceny 2016, done 2018 → 7-yr → eligible 2025. (3) 19-yr-old, qualifying misdemeanor, sentence done last month → 30-day petition — fastest conviction relief in the nation. (4) DUI misdemeanor 2014 → 10-yr → eligible. (5) conduct no longer criminal → immediate expungement.

**Verify:** 13 V.S.A. §§ 7601/7602 current text · vtcourts.gov expungement page (Act 60 summary) · VT Legal Aid clinic + fee numbers · qualifying-crimes list § 7601(4).

# 10. WYOMING — one shot each, priced by tier

**Misdemeanors (§ 7-13-1501):** **5 yrs** post-sentence (status offenses — underage-only crimes — **1 yr**); no firearm-use misdemeanors; healthcare-provider patient-care exclusions; "not a substantial danger" finding; **$100 filing fee**; 30-day prosecutor-objection window, then summary grant possible; **once per lifetime** under this section (subsection (k)) — bundle everything into the one petition. Notably: DV misdemeanors ARE expungable, and expungement lifts the federal firearm bar (CCRC flags it) — a branch that matters in Wyoming.

**Felonies (§ 7-13-1502):** **10 yrs** post-sentence; restitution paid in full; only felonies **from the same occurrence**; NO other felony history; excludes violent felonies (§ 6-1-104(a)(xii)), firearm felonies (except wildlife-code), sex crimes, child endangerment, felony DUI, drug-distribution ⚠️ full list from statute; **$300 filing fee**; **90-day** objection window; once per lifetime; **§ (m): expungement restores rights removed by the conviction** — including firearms.

**Non-convictions (§ 7-13-1401):** arrests/dismissals expungable **180 days** after arrest or dismissal, no charges pending ⚠️ confirm fee. Under-21 nicotine: auto-expunged 6 months post-fine (2020). Trafficking vacatur (2013). First-offender deferral (§ 7-13-301) avoids conviction entirely.

**Official self-help:** wyocourts.gov → Expungements (plain-language, confirms the rules above) — the small-state gem.

**Personas:** (1) misdemeanor 2018, done 2019 → 5-yr met → the one lifetime misdemeanor shot — bundle. (2) same-occurrence C felonies 2012, restitution paid → 10-yr met → $300, 90 days. (3) MIP at 19 (status) → 1-yr wait. (4) DV misdemeanor 2017 → expungable + firearms restoration — the WY-specific persona. (5) two felony convictions from different years → never; honest-no.

**Verify:** §§ 7-13-1401/-1501/-1502 current text · wyocourts.gov expungement page + forms · fee confirmation with a circuit clerk (Laramie or Natrona) · DCI transmittal step.
